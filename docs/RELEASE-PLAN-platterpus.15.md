# Release plan — `+platterpus.15`, the first release under R8

*Written 2026-09-23, the day round 25 closed. **A plan, not a release.**
`meson.build` says `0.9.4-rc2+platterpus.14`, the ledger's last row is seq 24,
and `tools/release-gate.py --release-gate` exits 0: round 25 closed `GO`/`GO` at
our lap 5 (`714f0a5`).*

**Nothing here is a decision still to make.** `.14`'s plan posed the channel as
a choice. This one does not, because round 25 settled it. Our lap 2 §B, written
on the operator's instruction, said *"We release `.15`, stable, with both
channels resolving to it"*. The operator chose *"Release lap 5 + cut .15"* after
reading Platterpus's lap 4. v6 R8 is the rule: a round's close authorises a
release of both applications, provider first, and both are usable.

## 1. The condition

- **The authorising round: 25**, closed `GO`/`GO` on 2026-09-23 at five laps.
  Our lap 5 transcribes Platterpus's lap 4 `GO` (sha256 `f6d18230…`).
- **No consumer-side prerequisite.** Unlike `.14`, `.15` removes no string a
  consumer matches on. Its two log changes add a form. The `Handshake:` draft
  qualifier appears only while a lap is held, so a released build never prints
  it. The second `Retry limit:` form appears only when `-r` is not a multiple
  of 5. Platterpus's parser reads both, by their lap 4 §B, with three tests at
  `platterpus@53b3c04:tests/test_golden_reference_parse.py`.

## 3. The channel: stable, and the window it opens

**Stable, both channels resolving to it.** Platterpus's app reads our manifest
and offers `.15` on its default channel. Their `FORK_PIN` must equal the pin of
their newest closed round, which is `3e01bb3`
(`platterpus@5374729:tests/test_fork_source.py:132-190`), so every rip made on
`.15` reports `unapproved` until round 26 reviews it. **That is the mark v6 R8
point 2 allows, and it is not withheld**: their offer states it, and a person
decides (`platterpus@5374729:src/platterpus/config.py:383-393`).

## 4. The sequence

1. **The gate** — `--release-gate` exits 0.
2. **Bump** `meson.build` to `0.9.4-rc2+platterpus.15`. Red by construction.
3. **Regenerate, never hand-edit**: the provider contract, the golden
   reference, the interrupted sample. Own commit, *"generated by X, committed
   at Y"*. Never `--amend`.
4. **Name the candidate** at the first commit where the version and every
   derived artifact agree. Prove it green on its own: the full suite in a fresh
   worktree from a removed log, and a `git archive` tarball built with
   `-Ddeclare_released=true` reporting `released build`.
5. **Publish**: one ledger row (seq 25, round 25), the manifest regenerated with
   `--check` exit 0, the changelog heading, the handshake README's pin blocks,
   `STATUS.md`'s release rows, and this file's banner, all in one commit.

No tag: tag push is `HTTP 403` here, and the commit SHA is the identifier.

## 5. What this release does NOT verify

- **Nothing in it has run on a drive.** The retry fix is proved by fault
  injection on a disc image (`tests/badsector.c`). How a real damaged disc
  fails — how slowly, and whether C2 says anything — is still untested. It is
  for the real test, which under R8 opens round 26.
- **At `-P 0`, one unreadable sector still hangs at any `-r`.** Not fixed, and
  in `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`. Platterpus never passes `-P`.
- **`.15` and 0.6.54 have never run together.** That is the real test.
- **The cache figure is still wrong** by roughly fifteen times. **Do not cite
  it.**
- **C2 stays `UNREACHABLE`** on the rig's drive. `-f` and CD-TEXT from a
  physical disc are *not yet done*, which is a different claim.
- **The loudness block is measured upstream of the filter graph**, and `File(s):`
  is still built from the request. Both are in `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.
